Avio.AI User Guide

12 NOV 2026 · Gabriel

Welcome Aboard

Avio.AI is a pocket-sized maintenance helper: you tell it what the airplane is doing, and it digs through your manuals faster than a crew chief looking for the last donut. It reads only your company's own manuals, so it never makes up a book that doesn't exist.

Think of it as a very fast, very polite research partner. It finds the right pages, explains them in plain words, and fills out your paperwork. It does not sign anything, fly anything, or decide anything. You are still the technician, and your signature still means what it always meant.

The one rule to remember: every answer comes with a citation (a manual, a chapter, a page). Tap it and check it. If the app and the manual ever disagree, the manual wins. Every time. No arguments.

Getting Set Up

Setup takes about five minutes, and most of it is pasting things your administrator sends you. The app itself is empty until you connect it to your company's data, kind of like a new phone before you sign in.

  1. Open the app in your phone's web browser at avio-ai.github.io/avio-app. On an iPhone, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, so it sits next to your other apps.
  2. If your administrator sent you a setup link, tap it. It fills in where your data lives. You still paste each access key yourself, because keys never travel inside a link.
  3. No link? Open Settings (the gear) and, under Connection, pick ＋ Add a Connection. Enter the data location and the access key you were given, then tap Save & Connect.
  4. Paste the Anthropic API Key in Settings. This is what wakes up the AI assistant. Without it, Search (manuals and wiring), MEL and PFI still work, but the chat stays quiet.
  5. Type your name under Technician Name. It shows on every case you start, so people know whom to thank (or whom to call).

Just want to look around first? Tap Try the Demo Aircraft. It is a made-up jet with made-up manuals, so you can't break anything real.

Keys are like house keys. Don't text them, email them, or tape them to the hangar fridge. If one leaks, tell your administrator so they can change it.

Picking Your Aircraft

Tap the aircraft name in the top bar to open the picker. Each aircraft type keeps its own manuals, cases and settings, so a hydraulic problem on one model never wanders into another model's notes.

  • Tails you've worked on show under each type. Tap one to jump right back in. Tap ✕ to remove it from the list (there's an Undo, for the oops moments).
  • Types you never touch can be hidden with ✕ too. Nothing is deleted; it just stops cluttering your screen. Show hidden aircraft brings them back.
  • Typed a tail under the wrong type? The app notices and offers Switch to the right aircraft. It's like autocorrect, but useful.

The app never fills in a tail number for you. You type it, so you always know which airplane you're working on.

Starting a Case and Chatting With the AI

A "case" is one problem on one airplane, from first squawk to final signature. The more you tell the app at the start, the less it has to guess.

  1. On the Mx Magic tab, fill in the tail number and serial number under Aircraft.
  2. Type the message exactly as it appears in the CAS / CMC Message or Fault Code box. The app suggests matches as you type.
  3. Under What's Happening?, describe it like you'd tell a friend: when it started, what you saw, what you already tried. "Same message on the last three legs" is gold.
  4. Tap Flight Phase & Conditions (Optional) to open it, then add the airport, when it happened, and the altitude, speed and temperature if you have them. Problems that only show up at 41,000 feet are very shy on the ramp.
  5. Tap Add Photo to take a picture or pick one from your phone's photo library. The AI can read the text on a screen or a part label.
  6. Tap Fix My Plane ▸. (It won't actually fix it. That part is still you.)

The AI answers with likely causes in order, the tests to run, and the manual pages that back it up. Reply in the chat box with what you found, like "connector was loose" or "reading was 28 volts", and it adjusts the plan. You can also tap the microphone to talk instead of type, which is handy when your hands are covered in hydraulic fluid.

Tap every citation. It opens the manual page so you can see it with your own eyes. The AI is smart, but the manual is the law.

Want to watch it think? While the AI works, its reasoning and every manual it looks up go into one Reasoning box, closed by default, with a (not entirely serious) status line on it while it works. When the answer is in, the box is labeled Reasoning. Tap the box to watch the steps live, or leave it closed and just read the answer when it lands below.

Switching cases mid-answer? If you open another case (or tap New) while the AI is still typing, the app stops that answer first, so nothing lands in the wrong case. No crossed wires, which is the whole point of this job.

Same case on two devices? If a teammate opens a case while the AI is still answering it on your phone, they see a note saying so, and their reply box waits until the answer is in. Then the answer shows up on their screen too, so nobody talks over each other (or over the robot).

If your company has connected the manual PDFs, a citation opens the real manual page with your words highlighted. Pinch to zoom, swipe left or right to turn the page, and tap Text for plain words. The demo jet has no PDFs, so it shows the text view:

Deferring With the MEL

The MEL (Minimum Equipment List) says what can be broken while the airplane still flies legally, and for how long. The MEL section of the RTS (Return To Service) tab looks it up for you; your company's own approved MEL still has the final say.

  1. Open the RTS tab, expand MEL, and pick your company's operating rule (for example Part 91 or Part 135). The rules change with it, so pick the right one.
  2. Enter the Day Of Discovery (the day the problem was found). The app counts the days and shows the repair-by date.
  3. Search for the broken item or the CAS message, in words or by MEL number.
  4. Read each relief option. Some items have several, labeled Option 1 of 2, and so on. Look for REQUIRED tags on the maintenance (M) and operations (O) steps.
  5. Tap Create Deferral PDF, or your company's discrepancy form if it has one set up.

During a case, the AI only records a deferral after the fault can't be fixed before the next flight, the relief conditions fit the airplane, and you choose to defer. A card then appears in the chat with the category and repair-by date. Think of the app as the friend who reminds you about the deadline, not the one who decides to skip the homework.

The repair-by date counts from the Day of Discovery on the card. It starts as the day you opened the case. If the squawk was found earlier, change it right on the card, or just tell the AI ("the crew wrote it up two days ago") and it sets the date for you. The repair-by date updates either way.

The RTS Tab

All the paperwork for a case lives in one place. Tap RTS (Return To Service) and expand what you need, in this order:

  • Component Transaction: every part removed and replaced on the case. Tap Add Component Transaction as many times as you need; each one keeps its own part and serial numbers.
  • Discrepancy log (your company's form, by its short name): fill it for the open case, or just the discrepancy.
  • MEL: look up relief and defer, as above.
  • Log Entry: one component log entry per transaction.
  • Repair tag: one tag per removed part, when your company has its tag set up.

No case open? Enter the tail and serial number and tap Start a Paperwork Case to do the paperwork without a troubleshooting chat. The app remembers which sections you keep open.

Wrapping Up a Case

When the problem is fixed, tell the AI in plain words, for example "replaced the valve, ops check good." It writes a log-style corrective action that cites the removal and test tasks it found. Check the wording before you use it; it's your entry, not the robot's.

  • Parts swapped? Give the part numbers and serial numbers off and on. The app records a component change and offers a Log Entry card. If your company's data includes the IPC, it can even look the part number up for you (see below).
  • Release paperwork: photograph the release certificate (8130-3 or similar) and the app reads the part numbers and dates off it. It flags anything that doesn't match what you typed, like a teacher with a red pen.
  • Forms: Create Log Entry PDF and, if your company uses one, the repair tag or discrepancy form. Sign on the screen with your finger, or leave the signature blank and sign the printout in ink. Either way, the signature is yours, not the robot's.
  • Case Brief: a one-page summary for Tech Ops, field reps and crew. Find it in the status bar at the top of the chat. Email it, save the PDF or copy the text.
  • Close Case: tap the status bar, then Close Case. Closed cases stay in Recent Cases, and you can reopen one if the problem comes back for a sequel.

All paperwork comes from the Export Case button too, along with the Full Report PDF for the whole step-by-step story.

Part numbers from the IPC: if your company's data includes the IPC (Illustrated Parts Catalog), the Component Log Entry looks up the part name you type and drops the best match into the empty P/N boxes. It searches your job's own chapter first, so a hose from the water system doesn't get mixed up with one from an engine. Each match shows the part number, its name and the IPC page. Tap a match to use it, or tap the page button to read the IPC page with the part number highlighted, then tap ‹ Log Entry to come right back with everything you typed still there. Already typed a P/N? The app checks it in the IPC and puts a ✔ on it when it's listed.

The IPC lists the part numbers that are approved, not the one that's actually bolted to your airplane. Always check the P/N against the data plate and fix the box if it's different. The robot reads catalogs; you read data plates.

Signing the log entry: near the bottom, under Work Performed By: Signature, sign with your finger. Or tap Import Signature to use a photo of your signature on white paper, or Use My Saved Signature if you've saved one before (on the PFI tab, for example). Clear starts over. When you tap Create Log Entry PDF, your signature lands on the Work Performed By line. Leave it blank and you sign the printout in ink, the old-fashioned way. If your company's repair tag has a signature box, the repair tag section gets its own Authorized Signature pad that works the same way.

The discrepancy log (your company's form, from the RTS tab or during a case) works the same way. Open Corrective Action, Parts, Return To Service, type the Part Name (IPC Look Up), and the P/N boxes fill in from the IPC. During a case, the discrepancy log and the Component Log Entry share one set of part and serial numbers, so change one and the other follows along. The form also has two signature pads: Deferred By: Signature for the deferral and Corrective Action: Signature for the fix. If your company's form has no spot set up for a signature, the app tells you, and you sign the printout.

Checklists on the PFI Tab

The PFI tab (short for pre-flight inspection, though it holds all your company's checklists) turns your company's checklists into tap-to-initial lists, then makes a neat one-page PDF. No more coffee rings on the walk-around card. The tab is always there, even before your company adds a checklist, because you can import your own (see below).

  1. Open PFI and pick a checklist at the top, like Post-Flight, Pre-Flight or CBs (circuit breakers). The title, the list buttons and the progress bar stay pinned at the top while you scroll, so you never lose your place. Edit and Import live up there too (more on those below).
  2. Fill in the Aircraft box: date, tail number, serial number and times. Type Your Initials once, in the last box, right next to APU Hours.
  3. Tap a line to initial it. Tap again for N/A. Tap a third time to clear it. Some lines have L and R boxes for the left and right sides, and some have boxes for numbers, like tire pressure.
  4. On servicing lines, type what you found and what you put in (or drained) in the Added boxes. That way the amount of oil you added ends up on the record, not just on your sleeve.
  5. At the bottom, type your name and certificate number and sign with your finger. Or tap Import Signature and pick a photo of your signature on white paper: the app removes the paper, keeps just the ink, and saves it on your phone. Next time, tap Use My Saved Signature.
  6. Tap Export PDF, then Download PDF to save or share it.
  7. Tap Save & Start New, then tap it again to confirm. The finished checklist goes into History and you get a fresh copy.

The bar at the top counts what's done ("2 of 9 initialed"), like a progress bar in a video game, except the prize is an airworthy airplane.

History: tap History at the top of the PFI tab to see every checklist saved for this aircraft, with the tail number, date, how far along it is and who initialed it. Tap Open to keep working on one or export it again. Tap ✕ twice to delete one.

Two technicians, one checklist: if your company turned on case sync in Settings, checklists are shared too. A teammate on another phone opens the same checklist from History, adds their initials and signs, and the PDF gets a signature row for each of you. With sync off, the checklist stays on your phone. The note under the signature box tells you which one you have.

Fixing a checklist: spot a typo or a missing line? Tap Edit at the top of any checklist. Every line shows up as plain text you can reword, add or delete. Tick I checked every line of this checklist, then tap Save Changes. With sync on, the fix reaches every technician on this aircraft type. With sync off, it stays on your phone. Changed your mind? Restore the Data Set Version (tap it twice) puts your company's original back.

Bringing in your own form: tap Import, then Choose PDF and pick your company's inspection form (an MSI, for example). The AI writes it out as a checklist, so this part needs the Anthropic API key. You check every line against the pictures of the original pages, fix anything it got wrong, tick the box and tap Approve & Add Checklist. Nobody can use it until you approve it. With sync on, everyone gets it. Later, Edit fixes a line or loads a new revision of the form, and Remove This Checklist (tap twice) takes it away for everyone.

The checklist on your phone is a helper. If it and the current manual ever disagree, the manual wins (you've heard that one before).

Case Email and Syncing Across Devices

Your cases can follow you from phone to tablet to laptop, and replies from Tech Ops can land right in the case. Your administrator turns these on once; after that they mostly run themselves.

  • Sync: in Settings, turn on Sync Case History Across Devices. Start a case on your phone, finish it on your tablet. A small cloud icon means a case was saved on another device and will download when you open it.
  • Case tag: every email the app writes has a tag in the subject, like [Case N123AB-K7Q]. Keep it in the subject when you forward or reply. It's the case's name tag at the party.
  • Replies: when Tech Ops answers, the app shows a banner like "2 case emails to file." Tap Review, check it matched the right case, then tap Import. Attachments come in as documents or photos, and the AI reads them and updates the case.
  • Notifications: tap Turn On Notifications in Settings → Case Email to get a ping on your phone when a reply arrives.

If an email lands on the wrong case, don't import it. Pick the right case in the review sheet first.

Getting Help and Sending Feedback

Stuck? Tap Help in the top bar (the question mark). Three buttons live there:

  • Quick Start Guide: setup and the basics in a few pages, for when you just need the short version.
  • User Guide: this guide, with every button explained. Tap Help at the top to go back, or Full Screen to read it on the whole screen (Exit Full Screen or Help brings you back).
  • Send Feedback: a direct line to the Avio.AI team.

Found a bug, missing a manual, or have an idea? Tap Help, then Send Feedback. Pick a type (Bug, Idea, Manual / Data, Checklist or Other), then type what happened or tap PTT and say it. Tap Add Screenshot if a picture helps, and leave your email or phone if you'd like a reply. Then tap Send Feedback.

It goes straight to the Avio.AI team. The app attaches your aircraft type, the tab you're on, your company's name and your device type, but never manual pages or case data. No signal? It waits on your phone and goes out later. Complaints about the coffee should still go to the crew chief.

Golden Rules and Quick Fixes

Five rules keep you and the airplane out of trouble:

  1. The manual wins. Check every citation. If the app and the manual disagree, follow the manual and tell your administrator.
  2. Your company's MEL wins over the master MEL shown in the app. It may be stricter.
  3. Enter the serial number. Wiring and some parts change from one airplane to the next.
  4. You sign, the app doesn't. Read every log entry and form before you put your name on it, in ink or on the screen. Check IPC part numbers against the data plate.
  5. Guard your keys. Never share access keys in a text, email or photo.
